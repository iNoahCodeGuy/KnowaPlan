# Lesson 7 revealed a one-pile money model, and a live "reversal is automatic" belief

Noah scored 1/4 on the three-accounts quiz (lessons/0009). The three
misses share a single root — he is tracking ONE pile of money, not three
accounts — and one of them, Q4, is the exact misconception that stranded
his two real transfers on 7/21. This is a re-ground, not a new topic.

**Evidence — his actual picks (read off the shown feedback):**

- **Q1** ("where is the un-reversed $1.66?") → *"Back on their card."*
  He collapses the refund and the transfer into one movement: card got
  its money back, so the money is "there." Misses that the transferred
  $1.66 is a SEPARATE pile still in the connected account.
- **Q2** ("who is out of pocket?") → *"The connected account."*
  Direction inverted. The connected account is the one holding EXTRA
  (the un-reversed transfer); the platform is short (paid the refund,
  never clawed the transfer back). **This is the cleanest signal — it
  was slot 4, an active content pick, not a first-option grab.**
- **Q4** ("what makes it reversed: true?") → *"Wait for it
  automatically."* The money one. He believes the reversal happens on
  its own. This is precisely the belief behind the 7/21 event
  (learning-records/0007): he refunded, assumed done, and both transfers
  still read reversed: false.
- **Q3** ("two planners — what breaks?") → *"Their account keeps it"* ✓.
  He owns the CONSEQUENCE ("you paid a stranger to refund your friend")
  even while missing the mechanism under it.

**Diagnosis — same seam, new territory.** This is the concept→mechanism
gap that records 0004/0005 established in the code, now showing up in the
money topology exactly where 0007 predicted the code lessons can't reach
(the topology lives in Stripe, not the repo). He holds the vivid
consequence (Q3) but not the substrate: which account physically holds
the money (Q1), which one is net-short (Q2), and whether the fix is
manual (Q4). Leading the lesson with the striking $30-stranger story may
have crowded out the three-piles mechanism it rests on — the memorable
part landed, the load-bearing part didn't.

**Caveat worth watching, not over-reading.** Three of four picks were
slot 1 (Q1, Q4, and the correct Q3). Not pure first-option anchoring —
Q2 was a deliberate slot-4 pick — but a mild first-option tendency can't
be ruled out from four questions. Q2 is the pick I'd trust most; it's
content-driven. Do not rebuild the curriculum on the slot pattern; do
confirm the re-teach with a fresh retrieval check.

**Implication for next.** Re-ground the three piles concretely before any
new topic: the refund and the transfer are two movements from two
accounts, so "money back on the card" (from the platform) and "transfer
still parked" (in the connected account) are both true at once. Land two
facts hard — (1) the platform is the one out of pocket because it paid
the share out twice (transfer + refund) and took it in once; (2) the
reversal is a manual, separate action, proven by his own two transfers
sitting at reversed: false since 7/21. Then re-check with a couple of
plain retrieval questions. The manual-reversal fact is money-critical for
7/29 and must be solid before the club night.
