Where the money actually goes

Lesson 2 · ~12 minutes · Your platform balance is negative right now, and your code is the reason

Lesson 1 was about reading a row. This one is about a thing no row will ever tell you: what a charge costs you.

Everything below came out of your own Stripe account on 2026-07-16. None of it is hypothetical, and none of it would have been caught by a test — your suite mocks Stripe, and a mock has no balance.

Start with the evidence

Three test charges. Here's what your platform's ledger says:

transfergross −$40.00 · fee $0.00 · net −$40.00
chargegross $40.00 · fee $1.46 · net $38.54
transfergross −$32.00 · fee $0.00 · net −$32.00
chargegross $32.00 · fee $1.23 · net $30.77
PLATFORM balanceavailable −$1.23 · pending −$1.46

Read the $40 as a story. Someone pays $40. Stripe keeps $1.46 and hands your platform $38.54. Then the transfer moves $40.00 — the full amount — to the planner's account. Your platform paid out forty dollars having received thirty-eight fifty-four.

It is down $1.46. That is why your balance reads negative.

This is not a test-mode quirk Test mode simulates the accounting exactly. In live mode, a negative platform balance is debited from your linked bank account.

Why: one missing parameter

This is the whole bug surface. Two optional parameters your code doesn't pass.

Here is the call, from app/payments.py:

transfer_data={
    "destination": planner.stripe_account_id,
},

There's no amount in there. Stripe's API reference says what that means:

“The amount that will be transferred automatically when a charge succeeds… if no amount is set, the full amount is transferred.”

And Charge types says who pays for it:

“You create a charge on your platform, so the payment appears in your platform's balance… Stripe debits fees from your platform's balance.”

Full amount out, fee charged to you. There's no third party absorbing it. The platform is you.

The trap I fell into — watch it, because you will too

Your account has a setting that reads like the answer: controller.fees.payer = "application", where “application” means your platform. It looks like the smoking gun. I called it the culprit. I was wrong, and the docs say so:

“Any activity occurring at the platform account level is billed to your platform regardless of the entity responsible for fee collection. For example, Stripe charges the platform directly for destination charges…” — Fee behavior on connected accounts

That setting governs direct charges. You make destination charges. Flipping it does nothing. Rebuilding as a Standard account does nothing either — the fee follows the charge type, not the account type.

The transferable lesson A plausible cause that sits right next to the symptom is the most expensive kind of wrong, because it survives review. Your own decisions.md does this: the 2026-07-16 success_url entry blames “a different Stripe account,” when the param is simply conditioned on ui_mode. The fix was right; the recorded reason was fiction. A wrong reason in an append-only log doesn't stay contained — it teaches everyone who reads it next.

Now the part that should bother you

Four of you. $120 court. You're playing, so you're a divisor but never a charge — decisions.md 2026-07-16. Everyone's share is $30.

Each friend is charged$30.00
You collect (3 × $30)$90.00
Stripe fees (3 × $1.17), from your platform−$3.51
You paid the court−$120.00
What you actually pay $33.51

Your friends pay $30.00. You pay $33.51.

And scenarios.md promises, in your own words:

“Everyone is charged floor(total cost / headcount); the planner absorbs the remainder — at most headcount − 1 cents per event. Nobody is ever charged a cent more than anyone else.”

You bounded that at 3 cents. Reality is 351 cents, every event, forever. You grilled the three-cent rule and shipped the three-hundred-fifty-one-cent one without noticing — not because you were careless, but because the fee lives outside the split math entirely. It isn't in settle_event. It isn't in any test. It's in a parameter you didn't pass, and it only becomes visible when you point the code at a real account and read the ledger.

That sentence is the honest answer to “how does this go into production.”

What you do about it — not today

You have a real decision here, and four ways to go: absorb it (status quo), transfer less than the share via transfer_data.amount, take it back via application_fee_amount, or gross the charge up so attendees cover it. Each has a different answer to “is it fair that the organizer pays extra?”

My recommendation: change nothing before 7/29 You are 13 days from a live event and the system works. Editing charge math now — with no auto-settle timer, no webhooks, and a suite that mocks Stripe and therefore cannot see fees at all — trades a known $3.51 for an unknown bug in the money path. That is a bad trade at any price. Record the decision, run the event, fix it after. What's urgent isn't the code; it's that scenarios.md currently states something false.

Charge types — Stripe

Read only the “Destination charges” section — about five minutes. It's the shape of your entire money path in one diagram, and it states both facts this lesson turns on: fees debit the platform, and so do disputes and refunds. That last one matters more than the fee. A single $30 dispute costs you the $30 plus a $15 dispute fee — and you'd be recovering it from your own connected account.

Ask me things Good ones for right now: “why is the platform a separate account from the connected one if they're both me?” · “what happens to the negative balance if I never fix it?” · “would application_fee_amount even do anything when I'm both sides?” That last one is sharper than it looks.
