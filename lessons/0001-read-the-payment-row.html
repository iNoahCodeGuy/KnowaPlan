Read the Payment row

Lesson 1 · ~10 minutes · The one skill you cannot be without on July 29th

It's 8pm on the 29th. The game is over, everyone's packing up paddles, and you've just settled the event on your phone. Marcus walks over: “Hey — did that go through? My bank texted me something weird.”

You have about ten seconds before this becomes awkward. You open the roster. One row tells you everything — if you can read it. That's this lesson. Not the whole money path; just the ability to look at one Payment row and say what happened to one person's money.

The shape of the thing

A Payment row has two kinds of column, and confusing them is how people lose money.

You designed this. decisions.md 2026-07-13, “Record-first money movement.” The lesson here is what it actually looks like in a row at 8pm.

What we tried — charge_requested_at and charge_requested_cents. Written before we call Stripe.

What we know — state and charged_cents. Written after Stripe answers.

That ordering is the entire design. Write down what you're about to do, then do it. Because there's a gap between those two writes, and your process can die in that gap — a crash, a timeout, a dropped wifi connection at a pickleball court.

The insight If the two halves disagree — we tried but we don't know — that's not a bug. That's the design working. It's called a dangling charge, and it is the one situation where you genuinely do not know whether money moved.

The five states

You wrote these. Quick refresher, because the quiz needs them loaded in memory:

noneNo charge yet. Also where no-shows stay forever.
paidMoney collected. The good one.
unpaidClose happened, we didn't collect — no card, or the card said no. Normal, not broken.
abandonedYou stopped chasing.
refundedA collected charge, reversed. (Not shipped yet.)

Two words you need first

PaymentIntent (Stripe's word, abbreviated pi_…): Stripe's record of one attempt to move money. Not a receipt. An attempt. It exists whether the attempt succeeded or the card was declined — the attempt happened either way, and Stripe wrote it down either way. This is the single most misread thing in the schema.

Off-session: charging a card when the human isn't sitting there. At close, Marcus is holding a paddle, not his phone — so his saved card gets charged off-session. Issuers are twitchier about off-session charges, which is exactly why they decline more often, and why the tap-to-pay link (where he is present) usually clears them.
