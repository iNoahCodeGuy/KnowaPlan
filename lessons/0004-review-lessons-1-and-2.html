Review — reading the row, and where the money goes

Interleaved retrieval · ~8 minutes · Lessons 1 and 2, shuffled together on purpose

These seven questions mix both lessons deliberately. Recalling "which column tells the truth" right after "who eats the fee" is harder than reviewing each lesson on its own — and that difficulty is the point. It's what moves this from I recognise it to I can pull it up cold at the court. One shot each. Commit before you tap; the wrong answers are where the teaching is.

1You want to know if one attendee's money actually moved. Which single column answers it?
Right — state is the verdict. charged_cents tells you how much, but state is the only column that says whether. Everything else is evidence.
No — stripe_payment_intent_id is the single biggest liar in the row. It's present on declines too. It's an attempt, never a receipt.
No — charge_requested_at means we were about to charge. Set ≠ charged. That's the whole dangling trap.
No — state_reason tells you why you landed somewhere, not whether money moved. It's a footnote to state.
2Marcus's $30 charge succeeds cleanly. Who ends up paying Stripe's ~$1.17 fee?
Right — Stripe debits the fee from your platform balance. $28.83 comes in, $30.00 transfers out, you're down $1.17. That's why your real balance reads negative.
No — Marcus pays exactly $30, the whole of which transfers to the planner. Grossing the charge up so the payer covers the fee is a change you haven't made yet.
No — the full $30 lands there, but the fee was already taken from the platform before the transfer. The connected account pays nothing.
No — Stripe keeps the fee; it doesn't absorb it. It takes it straight from the platform, which is you.
3You hit Settle, the page hung, you lost signal. The row now reads state=none, charge_requested_at set, charged_cents=NULL. What is it, and what do you do?
Right — stamp set, state still none: we told Stripe to take $30 and never heard back. Money may or may not have moved. Press Retry — same key, stamped amount, so Stripe replays the real outcome.
No — the expensive one — pristine needs the stamp NULL. Here it's set. Treating "we tried" as "nothing happened" is exactly how you double-charge Marcus.
No — a decline lands the row in unpaid with a reason. This row is none: Stripe never answered, so there's no decline to read.
No — collected means state=paid. You cannot claim collection from a row that never resolved. Retry and let Stripe tell you.
4Hoping the planner's account absorbs the fee, you rebuild it as a proper Standard account. What changes about who pays Stripe?
Right — the fee follows the charge type, not the account type. You make destination charges; those bill the platform "regardless of the entity responsible for fee collection." Standard changes your dashboard, not this fee.
No — but it's the trap I fell into — Standard does set fees.payer: account, which reads like the fix. It only governs direct charges. Yours are destination charges.
No — nothing about account type touches the rate. 2.9% + 30¢ either way.
No — no mechanism reimburses a platform for processing fees. The only fix is charging more or transferring less.
5Dave's row: state=unpaid, a pi_… id is present, state_reason=insufficient_funds. Did his money move?
Right — the pi_… is the record of the declined attempt, which is why we stored it: the DB must know everything Stripe knows. state=unpaid is the verdict. Text him the link; never re-fire the saved card.
No — the trap — you read pi_… as a receipt. It isn't. charged_cents is NULL and the reason says insufficient_funds. Only state says money moved.
No — unknown is dangling (state=none). Here you know exactly: the bank declined and told you why. This resolved.
No — there's no partial charge here. The attempt was declined outright; nothing collected.
6The whole $30 leaves your platform and you still eat the fee. Which single thing in the code makes the full amount transfer out?
Right — Stripe's API: "if no amount is set, the full amount is transferred." Your transfer_data passes only destination, no amount — so all $30 goes, and the fee hits the platform.
No — account type governs your dashboard and negative-balance liability, not how much transfers. That's Question 4's trap.
No — fees.payer is a direct-charge lever. It does nothing to a destination charge, and it isn't what sends the full amount out.
No — on_behalf_of sets the merchant of record; it's correct. It doesn't control the transfer amount.
7A clean settle finishes and half your roster sits at state=unpaid. What does that mean?
Right — unpaid is the normal resting state for anyone without a saved card (or a card that declined off-session). It means "the link is the path," not "something broke." Text them.
No — nothing failed. Cardless attendees are expected to land here; the system did exactly what it promised.
No — dangling is state=none with a stamp. These rows resolved to unpaid; retry would refuse them.
No — refunded reverses a collected charge. These were never collected; there's nothing to give back.
